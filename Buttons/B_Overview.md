Includes the files pertaining to the buttons printed onto the screen for the participant to press to answer the question posed in each trial/task. They were chosen as the method of data collection as it we cannot rely on the participants' verbal abilities, i.e., their ability to read/write to respond to questions. The buttons allow us to rely on their visual and auditory abilities instead.

Features of the buttons:
- Cartoonified to match the aesthetic
- Lights up when pressed
- Makes a sound when pressed
- Occupies approximately 2 cm x 2 cm of the screen
- Accompanied by text beneath indicating the answer pertaining to the particular button. 


**In the Exposure Phase: (distinguished by colour)**

Button 1:
- A circular green button
- Accompanied by a cheery "correct answer" sound
- Represents "yes"

Button 2: 
- A circular red button
- Accompanied by a cheery "correct answer" sound
- Respresents "no"

An advantage to working with these colours is that it is easy to form associations between them and the responses "yes" and "no", minimizing the participant's confusion about the answers represented by the buttons from interfering with their performance. A disadvantage is that the colour could also lead to an avoidance of the "no" button (this could also arise simply due to an aversion to "no" in general). This is offset/controlled by the incorporation of the same cheery sound effect into both the buttons. 

Certain trials in Training Phase 1 are also designed to minimize such undesirable reactions. More information about these trials can be found in TP1_Overview.md of the Training_Phase_1 directory. 

For more information on the Exposure_Phase, please refer to the Exposure_Phase directory.

**In the Test Phase: (distinguished by shape)**

Button 1:
- A purple square button
- Accompanied by a cheery "correct answer" sound if it represents the right answer for the particular trial, or an "incorrect answer" sound if it represents the wrong answer for the particular trial
- Represents "many" (if the displayed percentage in the gumball machine is 20/40/60/80) or "all" (if the displayed percentage in the gumball machine is 0/100)

Button 2:
- A purple circle button
- Accompanied by a cheery "correct answer" sound if it represents the right answer for the particular trial, or an "incorrect answer" sound if it represents the wrong answer for the particular trial
- Represents "few" (if the displayed percentage in the gumball machine is 20/40/60/80) or "none" (if the displayed percentage in the gumball machine is 0/100)

Button 3
- A purple triangle button
- Accompanied by a neutral sound
- Represents "not sure"

A third "not sure" button was added to prevent the participant from being forced to choose between two options. Shapes were used instead of colours as we couldn't find three equally distinguishable colours after red, green, blue and orange were used up (we didn't want to use red and green again as they represent different answers in the exposure phase). Incorrect and neutral sound effects are introduced to provide extra reinforcement and validation to the participant. Instructions are provided about the answers represented by the buttons for the first trial of the "many/few" category, and the first trial of the "all/none" category.

Development of the buttons:
- Adobe Fresco was used to create images of cartoon buttons in their states prior to and post selection (not lit up and lit up).
- Screen recordings were made of the layer containing the "lit up" effect being displayed (after a few seconds of being hidden) to make them seem animated.
- These screen recordings were combined with the sound effects in Microsoft Clipchamp.
